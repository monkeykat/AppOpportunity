# Phase 2 MVP: App Opportunity Investigator

## Goal

Phase 1 finds potentially interesting apps. Phase 2 investigates those apps to determine whether they represent a real software opportunity.

The goal is not to produce a perfect market research report. The goal is to answer:

> Is this app actually worth considering building a competitor or alternative to?

Phase 2 should investigate:

- The app itself
- Its users
- User complaints
- Competitors
- Alternatives and workarounds
- Demand
- Difficulty of building a competing product
- Proprietary data or technology requirements
- Likelihood of successfully competing

There is no frontend. Output is stored in a Phase 2-owned SQLite database.

## Phase Boundary

Phase 1 in the `Scout` folder is complete and must remain completely unchanged.

Phase 2 is a separate implementation in the `Investigator` folder:

- Do not modify, overwrite, migrate, or extend any file under `Scout`.
- Do not import Phase 1 Python modules at runtime.
- Do not read from or write to the Phase 1 database during normal Phase 2 execution.
- Recreate the minimum required support modules and database schema inside `Investigator`.
- Transfer Phase 1 opportunity records to Phase 2 through an explicit copy or export/import handoff.
- Treat the Phase 2 database as the only database Phase 2 reads or writes.

## Technology

Use:

- Python
- SQLite
- Ollama
- Playwright

Web search may be used where necessary to research publicly available information.

Keep dependencies minimal. Do not build a complex multi-agent system.

## Database

Create and use a separate database:

```text
app_investigator.db
```

The Phase 2 database must contain a recreated, minimal `opportunities` table with the fields needed to select and investigate opportunities. Its records are populated through the Phase 1-to-Phase 2 handoff.

Do not redesign or modify Phase 1. Phase 2 should create its own:

- Phase 1-compatible `opportunities` table
- `investigations` table

The Phase 2 `investigations` table is separate from any Phase 1 database:

```text
investigations
```

### `investigations`

Stores the results of the detailed investigation.

Fields:

```text
id
app_id
status
app_analysis
user_analysis
complaint_analysis
competitor_analysis
alternative_analysis
build_difficulty
proprietary_dependency
market_potential
competition_level
final_score
recommendation
summary
investigated_at
```

One app should have only one investigation.

### `status`

Possible values:

```text
PENDING
IN_PROGRESS
COMPLETE
FAILED
```

### Analysis Fields

The following fields can store text generated by Ollama:

```text
app_analysis
user_analysis
complaint_analysis
competitor_analysis
alternative_analysis
```

These do not need to be complicated. They should contain concise summaries.

### Scoring Fields

Use simple scores from 1 to 10:

```text
build_difficulty
proprietary_dependency
market_potential
competition_level
final_score
```

### `recommendation`

Possible values:

```text
PASS
INVESTIGATE_FURTHER
PROMISING
STRONG_OPPORTUNITY
```

## Selecting an Opportunity

Phase 2 should select an opportunity in its own database that originated from Phase 1 and has not yet been investigated.

Example:

```sql
SELECT opportunities.*
FROM opportunities
LEFT JOIN investigations
    ON opportunities.app_id = investigations.app_id
WHERE investigations.id IS NULL
ORDER BY opportunities.score DESC
LIMIT 1;
```

This means:

```text
Highest Phase 1 Opportunity copied into Phase 2
        |
        v
Investigate
        |
        v
Next Highest Opportunity
        |
        v
Investigate
```

Do not investigate the same app twice.

# Investigation Steps

## Step 1: Study the App

Collect additional publicly available information about the app.

Use:

- Google Play
- The developer's website, if available
- Public app descriptions
- Public documentation, if available

Determine:

- What does the app actually do?
- What problem does it solve?
- Who is it designed for?
- What are its primary features?
- What makes it different from similar apps?
- What information or services does it depend on?

Examples of dependencies:

- Public weather data
- Maps
- GPS
- Government data
- User-generated content
- Private databases
- Specialized hardware
- Proprietary information
- Professional licenses

Create a concise `app_analysis`.

Example:

> The app helps recreational fishermen track catches, locations, weather conditions, and fishing history. Its core functionality appears relatively straightforward. The primary external dependencies are GPS and weather data.

## Step 2: Study the Users

Determine who uses the app.

Look for evidence from:

- Google Play reviews
- Reddit
- Public forums
- Relevant communities
- App descriptions and marketing

Try to answer:

- Who uses this?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- How serious is the problem?
- How often do they need to solve it?
- Are they hobbyists or professionals?
- Would they potentially pay for a better solution?

Do not try to build a perfect demographic profile. The goal is simply to understand:

> Who would use a competing product and why?

Create a `user_analysis`.

## Step 3: Study User Complaints

This is one of the most important parts of Phase 2.

Collect a reasonable sample of public user feedback.

Prioritize:

- 1-star reviews
- 2-star reviews
- 3-star reviews

Also collect some positive reviews and look for repeated patterns.

Examples of complaints:

- Too many ads
- Crashes frequently
- Outdated interface
- Missing important feature
- Subscription too expensive
- Poor customer support
- Data loss
- Difficult to use
- Developer abandoned the app

The goal is not to find one angry person. The goal is to find repeated complaints.

Feed the collected feedback to Ollama. Ask it to identify:

- Repeated complaints
- Most serious complaints
- Missing features
- Potential product improvements
- Complaints that appear solvable

Store the result in `complaint_analysis`.

## Step 4: Study Competitors

Search for competing apps.

Look at:

- Google Play
- Relevant websites
- Public web search

Identify approximately 3-10 competitors. A complete market analysis is not necessary.

For each competitor, try to collect:

- Name
- What it does
- Rating, if available
- Approximate popularity, if available
- Major strengths
- Major weaknesses

The important questions are:

- Is this market already crowded?
- Are there several strong competitors?
- Are all competitors basically bad?
- Does one company dominate?
- Are there multiple small competitors?

Create a `competitor_analysis`.

## Step 5: Study Alternatives and Workarounds

Competitors are not always apps.

Ask:

> How do people solve this problem if they do not use this app?

Look for:

- Spreadsheets
- Paper notebooks
- Facebook groups
- Reddit communities
- Manual processes
- Generic software
- Websites
- YouTube
- Multiple separate apps
- Physical tools
- Doing nothing

This can reveal opportunities.

Example:

```text
Problem:
Track fishing catches.

Existing app:
Fishing Tracker.

Alternative:
People use:
- Notes
- Spreadsheets
- Photos
- Google Maps
```

That might indicate the existing software is not solving the problem well.

Create an `alternative_analysis`.

## Step 6: Evaluate Build Difficulty

Determine how difficult it would be to build a reasonable competing MVP.

Do not evaluate how difficult it would be to perfectly clone the existing app.

Evaluate:

- Number of major features
- Technical complexity
- Backend requirements
- Third-party APIs
- Mobile development complexity
- AI requirements
- Real-time requirements
- Specialized hardware
- Data requirements

Return a score:

```text
1 = Very easy
5 = Moderate
10 = Extremely difficult
```

Example of a simple product:

```text
Fishing log
GPS
Photos
Basic statistics

Score: 3
```

Example of a difficult product:

```text
Real-time navigation
Massive proprietary maps
Hardware integration
Live global data

Score: 9
```

Store the result in `build_difficulty`.

## Step 7: Evaluate Proprietary Dependencies

Determine whether the app depends on something difficult to reproduce.

Examples:

- Proprietary datasets
- Exclusive partnerships
- Large private databases
- Network effects
- User-generated content
- Specialized hardware
- Patented technology
- Professional expertise
- Regulated information

Return a score:

```text
1 = No meaningful proprietary dependency
10 = Extremely difficult to reproduce
```

Examples of low dependency:

- GPS
- Public weather API
- User-entered information

Score: 2

Example of high dependency:

- Exclusive access to millions of private records

Score: 9

Store the result in `proprietary_dependency`.

## Step 8: Evaluate Market Potential

Use available evidence to estimate whether there is a real market.

Consider:

- Install counts
- Review counts
- Number of competitors
- Community activity
- Search results
- User discussions
- Evidence of people paying for alternatives
- Repeated user demand

Return a score:

```text
1 = Very little apparent demand
5 = Moderate potential
10 = Strong evidence of demand
```

Store the result in `market_potential`.

This is an estimate. Do not pretend it is a precise market size calculation.

## Step 9: Evaluate Competition

Determine how difficult it would be to compete.

Consider:

- Number of competitors
- Quality of competitors
- Popularity of competitors
- Dominant companies
- Switching costs
- Network effects

Return a score:

```text
1 = Very little competition
5 = Moderate competition
10 = Extremely competitive
```

Store the result in `competition_level`.

## Step 10: Final Analysis

After all research is collected, send everything to Ollama.

The goal is to answer:

> Should this idea be seriously considered?

Use a prompt similar to:

```text
You are evaluating a potential software opportunity.

You have researched an existing Android app and its market.

Your job is to determine whether creating a competing or improved product appears worth considering.

Do not assume that a poorly rated app is automatically a good opportunity.

Consider:
- Evidence of demand
- Size of the apparent audience
- User complaints
- Whether complaints are repeated
- Whether complaints are solvable
- Existing competitors
- Alternative solutions
- Difficulty of building a useful competing product
- Proprietary barriers
- Competition
- Potential ability to differentiate

Return only valid JSON:
{
    "final_score": 1-10,
    "recommendation": "PASS | INVESTIGATE_FURTHER | PROMISING | STRONG_OPPORTUNITY",
    "summary": "Concise explanation of the opportunity and major risks."
}
```

Save the returned values to:

- `final_score`
- `recommendation`
- `summary`

# Final Score

The final score should be 1-10.

## 1-3: `PASS`

Little evidence of a worthwhile opportunity.

## 4-5: `INVESTIGATE_FURTHER`

Some interesting signals, but insufficient evidence.

## 6-7: `PROMISING`

Evidence of a potential opportunity. Worth considering.

## 8-10: `STRONG_OPPORTUNITY`

Strong evidence of:

- Demand
- Solvable problems
- An achievable product
- A reasonable competitive opportunity

These should be rare. Do not allow Ollama to give everything an 8.

# Running the Application

Run:

```bash
python investigate.py
```

Each run should:

1. Select one uninvestigated opportunity.
2. Create an investigation record.
3. Research the app.
4. Research its users.
5. Collect and analyze complaints.
6. Find competitors.
7. Find alternatives and workarounds.
8. Evaluate build difficulty.
9. Evaluate proprietary dependencies.
10. Estimate market potential.
11. Estimate competition.
12. Ask Ollama for a final assessment.
13. Save the investigation.
14. Exit.
